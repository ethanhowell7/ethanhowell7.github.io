Leadership

Founding a team, teaching a skill, and running a business — the same instinct to take ownership and build systems, applied in three different places.

Photo

Founding and mentoring ARC Sandstorm

Role: Founder & mentor Team: ARC Sandstorm, FTC #24256 Result: State & NEI finish, first season

I founded ARC Sandstorm, an FTC robotics team, after two years competing on ARC Lightning. In our first competitive season we made it to states and an NEI finish. I also ran "ARC Election '26," a real leadership election for team positions using both simple and ranked-choice ballots, and closed the season with a banquet and nature-themed awards.

The decision I keep coming back to from that season: I spent time building up newer members' skills instead of routing every hard problem through our most experienced people. Slower, and it probably cost us some competitive ground early on, but it's the only way the team still works once I'm not the one running it.

Teaching karate

Rank: Black belt Experience: Several years teaching

I hold a black belt in karate and have taught it for several years, which is where a lot of my approach to coaching and leadership actually comes from. My job as an instructor isn't really getting a student to copy a technique correctly — it's giving them a way to figure out what "correct" means on their own, so they keep improving without me standing over their shoulder. That's the same approach I bring to mentoring on the robotics team.

Designing a STEM curriculum for a martial arts dojo

Format: 4-day summer camp Location: Andover, MA Status: Delivered

I designed and ran a four-day robotics-and-karate summer camp at the dojo where I teach, building the curriculum from scratch: daily lesson plans on solar energy, gears, biomimicry, and the engineering design loop, each with its own slide deck and diagrams I drew myself.

Building the lesson decks and diagrams took longer than actually running the camp — probably true of teaching in general. It's still the single project that best represents how I think about both fields at once.

Photo

Running a DJ business

Type: Self-employed Area: Greater Boston Status: Active

I run my own event DJ business around the Greater Boston area — booking clients, managing gigs, and tracking the business itself with a financial system I built covering income, expenses, mileage, and a dashboard that rolls everything up by year.

Most of what I've actually learned running it has been operational rather than musical — pricing right, keeping equipment and mileage costs from quietly eating a slow month. It's the same instinct I bring to an engineering problem, just pointed at a spreadsheet instead of a circuit.
