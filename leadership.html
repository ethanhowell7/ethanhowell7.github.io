Leadership

Leading a team, teaching a skill, and running a business — the same instinct to take ownership, applied in three different places.

Four years of competitive robotics

Two teams, two very different roles: three years learning the engineering side, then a year responsible for the people doing it.

Freshman – junior year

ARC Lightning, FTC #4410 — mechanical lead

Three seasons on the engineering side, leading mechanical design on drivetrain and scoring mechanisms. Our junior-year season took us to the FTC World Championship.

Senior year

ARC Sandstorm, FTC #24256 — team leader

Moving from mechanical lead to team leader meant the problems stopped being technical. I led the team to a states and NEI finish, ran "ARC Election '26" for team leadership positions using both simple and ranked-choice ballots, and closed the season with a banquet and nature-themed awards.

The decision I keep coming back to: I spent time building up newer members' skills instead of routing every hard problem through our most experienced people. Slower, and it probably cost us some ground early on, but it's the only version where the team still works once I've graduated.

Teaching karate

Rank: Black belt Experience: Several years instructing

I hold a black belt in karate and have taught it for several years, which is where most of my approach to coaching comes from. My job as an instructor isn't really getting a student to copy a technique correctly — it's giving them a way to judge their own form, so they keep improving without me standing over their shoulder. That's the same thing I was trying to do leading Sandstorm.

Featured work

Designing a STEM curriculum for a martial arts dojo

Format: 4-day summer camp Location: Andover, MA Status: Delivered

I designed and ran a four-day robotics-and-karate summer camp at the dojo where I teach, building the curriculum from scratch: daily lesson plans on solar energy, gears, biomimicry, and the engineering design loop, each with its own slide deck and diagrams I drew myself.

Building the lesson material took considerably longer than running the camp did — probably true of teaching in general. It's still the project that best represents how I think about both fields at once.

Running a DJ business

Type: Self-employed Area: Greater Boston Status: Active

I run my own event DJ business around the Greater Boston area — booking clients, managing gigs, and tracking the business itself with a system I built covering income, expenses, mileage, and a dashboard that rolls everything up by year.

Most of what I've learned running it has been operational rather than musical: pricing the work properly, and keeping equipment and travel costs from quietly eating a slow month. Same instinct I'd bring to an engineering problem, pointed at a spreadsheet instead of a circuit.
