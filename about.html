About

I'm a first-year electrical engineering student at NC State University, in the University Honors Program. I grew up in Andover, Massachusetts, and split my time between Raleigh during the school year and Andover in the summers.

Outside of class, I hold a black belt in karate and have taught it for several years, I founded and mentor an FTC robotics team, and I run a self-employed DJ business around the Greater Boston area. Electrical engineering is the one that stuck after AP Physics C and a lot of time soldering guitar pedals — I like problems where you can actually see and measure what's happening, and where getting something wrong usually tells you exactly what you got wrong.

Philosophy

Five principles from martial arts training that I use to navigate decisions that don't have an obviously correct answer.

Effort

Showing up consistently matters more than any single result, whether that's a scored match or a competition deadline.

Etiquette

How you treat people when you don't have to says more about your actual standards than what you claim them to be.

Sincerity

Give people a straightforward answer, even when a vaguer one would be easier in the moment.

Self-control

Staying level-headed under pressure — a match, a deadline, a circuit that won't behave — is a practiced skill, not a personality trait.

Character

What you do without anyone watching or scoring it is the real measure.

The clearest example: choosing to build up newer teammates' skills on ARC Sandstorm instead of routing every hard problem through our most experienced people, even when it cost us some competitive ground in the short term.

Goals

Near term, that means landing a summer 2027 engineering internship and finding a way into semiconductor device research here at NC State. Further out, I'm interested in power electronics and device work specifically — the kind of engineering where a small design choice has a physical, measurable consequence.
