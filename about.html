Who I am

About

Where I'm from, how I work, and what I'm doing right now.

I'm a first-year electrical engineering student at NC State University, in the University Honors Program. I grew up in Andover, Massachusetts, and split my time between Raleigh during the school year and Andover over the summers.

I spent four years in competitive FTC robotics — three on the mechanical side of ARC Lightning, then a year as team leader of ARC Sandstorm. I hold a black belt in karate and teach at the dojo where I trained, worked several jobs at the Cormier Youth Center, and sat on the executive board of a 200-member National Honor Society chapter. I also run a self-employed DJ business around Greater Boston, which has taught me more about running things than any class has.

Electrical engineering is what stuck after AP Physics C and a lot of time soldering guitar pedals. I like problems where you can see and measure what's happening, and where being wrong usually tells you exactly how you were wrong. Power devices and semiconductor work are where that instinct is pointing right now.

Outside of engineering, French matters to me. I've studied it for years, hold the Massachusetts Seal of Biliteracy, and try to keep it up — it's a different way of thinking, not just a different set of words.

How I work

Three things I've actually learned from doing the things above, rather than principles I signed up for.

Build capability, not just results

On Sandstorm I chose to develop newer members instead of routing everything through the strongest people. It cost us in the short term. It's still the right call, because a team that only works with its best member present isn't a team.

Teach the judgment, not the answer

Whether it's a karate student or a robotics rookie, the goal is that they can evaluate their own work. If they still need me to tell them whether it's right, I haven't finished teaching.

Learn by building the thing

Pedals, robots, a note-drilling app, two apps built in middle school with a friend — I understand something once I've made a version of it and watched it fail. Reading about it comes second.

Off the clock

Electric guitar, mostly — playing it and building the effects for it, which is where the pedal projects come from. I've been getting into songwriting. And a fair amount of the DJ business is just an excuse to spend time with music from the other side of the table.

Rock climbing is the other one. I'm top-rope and lead certified at Central Rock Gym, and I like it for the same reason I like debugging a circuit: the wall gives you honest feedback, immediately, and the only way up is to actually solve the problem in front of you.
